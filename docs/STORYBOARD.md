# Behind a Better Life — storyboard (v2)

One presenter-driven animated deck built from two earlier cuts:

- **Story Cycle** (the Nouf / Faisal film): strong human story, one continuous world, weak business case.
- **Executive Cut** (the final PowerPoint content in 17 camera stations): complete business case, but clicks often landed mid-flight on empty frames, the type was small for a room, and it needed an internet connection.

v2 folds in a three-lens review (committee persuasion, motion design, live-event risk). The ask now shows up early and is concrete. Duplicate beats are cut. The cycle has one name. The illustrative story carries no invented numbers. Every scene has its own layout shape. The deck ends on the decision.

## The idea that ties it together — a story is a light
A single teal light (a rounded square taken from the pixels of the Tahakom mark) stands for one colleague's story. Behind everything is a quiet, uncountable **colleague field**. Its dim squares run off every edge, so it never implies a headcount. The light changes state in order, and each state happens once:

| State | Where | What the light does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spark | 01 | One light ignites. |
| Held | 02 | Four lights, one per moment, then three sink back into the field. |
| Contained | 03 | The camera pulls back. Two lit squares — Nouf and the colleague next to her — in a vast dark field. |
| Walled | 04 | Light is cut off at the district walls; the bridges fail. |
| Counted | 05 | 158 squares, one per respondent. |
| Carried | 09 | The light orbits the cycle. |
| Multiplied | 10 | For the first time the light divides: Nouf nominates the next colleague. |
| Steady | 14 | The light runs a measurement lane that never hits a wall. |
| Chain | 17 | Starting from the exact two squares of 03, light passes colleague to colleague until the field is lit. |

Nothing lights more than about 10% of the field before scene 17.

## Live rules
- Every press moves exactly one stop. A press during a build settles that build and plays the next one.
- All the **text** for a stop lands within about 1.2 s of the click. Motion may continue after that.
- Back lands on the previous stop's settled frame.
- While parked, the frame stays alive but calm: one main ambient motion per frame, no counters ticking, nothing that looks like a new build.
- Colour roles:
  - teal: stories and strengths
  - purple (`#9D67AA` / `#C9A6D3`): gaps and "the old way"; never dark plum on navy
  - sea green: at most one hero element per frame
  - queen blue / 60% white: benchmarks and neutral values
- Type minimums: sentences 30px+, labels 22px+, caveats and sources 24px at 80% white.

Ambient: stops 0–2, the clock colon blinks and the photo's screens glow. Stop 3: only the pair breathes.

## II · THE GAP

### 04 · The gap — district map — 5 stops
Three team "districts" across the lower half of the frame: rounded boundaries, each holding a cluster of squares.
- **0** `THE GAP` · **Inspiration is happening — but it is not always visible.** · *Positive behaviours happen every day, yet many are not consistently recognised or shared across Tahakom.*
- **1** 01 **Good work happens** — *Employees help, solve problems and go the extra mile in their daily work.* Lights ignite inside each district.
- **2** 02 **Visibility stays local** — *These contributions are often noticed only by the immediate team or manager.* Ripples expand and stop hard at each wall (purple wall flash).
- **3** 03 **Learning does not travel** — *Useful behaviours are not consistently shared across departments or repeated.* Dashed bridges between districts: a light sets out and fades halfway.
- **4** Districts recede. *Meaningful contributions exist — the gap is making them visible, recognised and shared across Tahakom.* **Behind a Better Life closes that gap through real employee stories.** Then a small strip: `TODAY'S ASK · Approve a one-quarter pilot`.
Ambient: at most one event every 6–8 s — a ripple hitting a wall, or a bridge light failing.

- **3** **49%** PRACTICAL LEARNING (teal) — *Say a colleague's story gives them an approach they can use.* 77 squares teal.

### 06 · Reading the numbers — dumbbell chart, full width — 4 stops

- **0** `READING THE NUMBERS` · **Willingness is strong; the mechanism is weak.** The axis draws. *Directional comparison only: measures and populations are not identical.* (28px, under the axis, on every stop.) Legend: Tahakom · Benchmark.
- **1** THE MECHANISM (purple; the Tahakom dot sits left of the benchmark): ORG-WIDE RECOGNITION 8% vs 22% **−14 pts** · CHOICE IN RECOGNITION 49% vs 64% **−15 pts**
- **2** THE WILLINGNESS (teal; the Tahakom dot sits right): PEER RECOGNITION 89% vs 41% **+48 pts** (sea-green hero) · CONNECTION / LOYALTY 80% vs 77% **+3 pts** *broadly aligned*

Ambient: a small light travels each dumbbell segment slowly; the dots breathe.

## IV · THE CYCLE

### 09 · The cycle — two lanes become a ring — 3 stops
- **0** `WHAT IS NEW` · **Most recognition stops at the award. This keeps going.** Two lanes: a purple lane *Achievement → Recognition* whose light stops at a wall (*it ends here*), and a teal lane *Experience → Story → Behaviour → Inspiration → More stories* whose light loops back to its start.
- **1** The teal lane curls into a ring (the lanes fade and the ring draws). `THE SOLUTION` **A simple story-to-impact cycle.** Four nodes land in one timed sweep:
  - 01 **CAPTURE** *Peer or leader nomination through a short, clear form.*
  - 02 **CURATE** *Verify facts, secure consent and link the story to one value.*
  - 03 **FEATURE** *Publish a short, authentic story across relevant channels.*
  - 04 **REINFORCE** *Recognise the contribution, share the takeaway and track response.*
- **2** **THE CYCLE REPEATS** at the centre. The design principle appears as small subtitles under the four nodes: *employee voice · fair selection · visible recognition · organisational learning*.
Ambient: the story light orbits the ring without stopping; each node pulses as it passes.

## V · THE PILOT

- **0** `PILOT OUTCOME` **Measure whether visibility becomes behaviour.** *The pilot should prove participation, reach, recognition and repeatable learning.*

- **2** A strip: `BASELINE → AFTER ONE QUARTER` — *Recognition reach 8% today · visibility gap 56% today → same eight questions, re-asked at quarter end.* **Quarterly dashboard · trend review · design improvements**
Ambient: the light keeps cycling the lane slowly.

### 15 · What could break this — risk rows with owners — 2 stops
- **0** `RISKS, DESIGNED IN` **What could break this — and what already guards against it.** Four rows, risk → mitigation already in the design → owner:
  - *It becomes a popularity contest or a leadership broadcast* → Peers nominate; Featured Story criteria are published → Curation panel
  - *Nominations dry up after launch* → Nominations always open; every featured colleague nominates the next → Internal Communications
  - *A sensitive or contested story is published* → Facts checked and the employee's consent before anything is shared → HR, with Legal on request
  - *Stories cluster in two departments* → Representation tracked from month one → HR / People Analytics
- **1** *Owners to be confirmed with each team before launch.* **Every mitigation is something you have already seen in the design.** (skippable)
Ambient: a slow scan light down the rows.

`team-ops.jpg` under a heavy veil. The ask is set in the dark ceiling band, not over the bright video wall.

## Honesty rules
- Nouf and Faisal are **illustrative**. Every frame that shows them carries the `ILLUSTRATIVE STORY` tag, from 02.1 on, where the presenter first says so.
- The illustrative story uses no invented numbers (no minutes saved, no reach counts), and there are no headcounts anywhere.
- Survey and benchmark figures appear exactly as in the final deck, with the directional caveat legible on screen.
- Retention is framed as a possible long-term effect, never as a pilot outcome.
- The risk owners (15) and the "what we need" list (16) are new proposals built from the team's own concept deck. They are flagged in the notes for the team to confirm.
